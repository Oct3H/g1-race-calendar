Going to Sky Stands in Kikuka Sho (Japanese St. Leger)
Amid high expectations of a Triple Crown, third favorite
Going to Sky
, bouncing back from a sixth-place finish in the St. Lite Kinen (G2, 2,200m) on September 13, captured the final leg of the Triple Crown, the Kikuka Sho (Japanese St. Leger), denying double-crown champion Lovcen the sweep. Sired by 2020 Triple Crown winner Contrail, the bay colt won his debut race in October (Tokyo, 2,000m) before registering a third in the Kyoto Nisai Stakes (G3, 2,000m) the next month. After finishing sixth in the Kisaragi Sho (G3, 1,800m) this February, he grabbed his first graded title in the Aoba Sho (G2, 2,400m) in April, which was followed by a fourth in the Tokyo Yushun (Japanese Derby, G1, 2,400m). This victory marked the first G1 title for both his sire and trainer Yuki Uehara who opened his yard in 2023 and had scored four grade-races prior to the Kikuka Sho. Jockey Yutaka Take won his 87th JRA-G1 title, his first since this year’s Takarazuka Kinen aboard Meisho Tabaru, and sixth Kikuka Sho triumph after Super Creek (1988), Dance in the Dark (1996), Air Shakur (2000), Deep Impact (2005) and World Premiere (2019). He set a new record for the most Kikuka Sho victories.
Going to Sky
broke smoothly and took a ground-saving trip in mid-pack while
King Glory
gunned for the lead to set the pace, widely distancing himself with the rest of the field. The frontrunner’s huge lead of nearly 20 lengths in the backstretch gradually dwindled as the field rounded the final corners. Entering the straight, the Contrail colt was shifted a few paths off the rail, advanced through an opening to take command 100 meters out and surged to the wire, narrowly holding off the runner-up’s late charge to prevail by a nose.
“I knew there were some strong horses in the field, but I wanted to focus on riding my own race. I’ve ridden this colt several times now, and I’d given a lot of thought to how best to ride him. He settled nicely in a good spot along the rail, and I felt we were in a very good rhythm. I decided to wait and make our move in the stretch. He responded well once given a green light and with about 200 meters to go, I thought we might win. He ran a good race in the Derby, and I felt we had a good chance in the last leg of the Triple Crown,” commented Yutaka Take.
Eighth pick
Basse Terre
was unhurried from the start, traveling near the rear up to the final turn. Heading for home with the fastest last three-furlong speed, the Kitasan Black colt kicked into gear bumping a rival in early stretch, then squeezed through a gap while briefly blocking the race favorite and unleashed a strong drive to the wire to threaten the eventual winner for the runner-up spot.
Second choice
Danon History
traveled most of the distance wide around sixth and gradually improved position to enter the lane in third. Battling with rivals for the lead, the son of Epiphaneia was overtaken by the eventual winner on the inside and runner-up from the outside in the last 200 meters but found another gear in the final strides to secure third place while fending off the fast-closing Sanono Greater by half a length.
Heavily favored
Lovcen
broke poorly and was forced to sit among the trailing runners, fourth to fifth from the rear. Attempting to make up ground, the colt made rapid headway from the outside before the third corner and although hitting the stretch in good striking position, the star colt could not match the charge of his rivals and faded to 14th.

* Kikuka Sho (Japanese St. Leger) (G1)
The history of Kikuka Sho, the Japanese St. Leger and the last leg of the Triple Crown race for three-year-old colts and fillies, dates back to 1938—a year after Japan’s 11 racing clubs integrated into one organization named Nihon Keiba Kai (forerunner of the JRA). Initially called “Kyoto Norin-sho Shoten Yonsai Yobiuma Kyoso”, the race was renamed to Kikuka Sho in 1948. The race was held at its temporary venue of Hanshin Racecourse in 2021 and 2022 while Kyoto Racecourse was undergoing renovation.
The Kikuka Sho features three-year-olds, most of who have matured dramatically during the five months following the Tokyo Yushun (Japanese Derby, G1, 2,400m) and their summer break. The Kikuka Sho winners have both the speed and the stamina to prove their caliber as potential breeding stock. The last of the eight Triple Crown winners in JRA history was Contrail (JPN, by Deep Impact) who followed in the footsteps of his sire by accomplishing the feat undefeated in 2020.
In the spotlight this year was
Lovcen
, the double crown champion one race away from the Triple Crown sweep. The World Premiere colt came into the race from a narrow win in the Kobe Shimbun Hai (G2, 2,400m, Sep.21) where
Sea to Summit
followed in a close third. Claiming the other trial race, the St. Lite Kinen (G2, 2,200m, Sep.13), was
Justin Chicago
with the Spring Stakes (G2, 1,800m) victor
Audacia
in third. The last leg of the Classics also included
Going to Sky
,
Congestus
,
Sanono Greater
and
Danon History
, respective winners of the Aoba Sho (G2, 2,400m), the Kyoto Shimbun Hai (G2, 2,200m), the Radio Nikkei Sho (G3, 1,800m) and the Akanko Tokubetsu (2 Wins Class, 2,600m, Aug.16), as well as
Ask Edinburgh
and
Basse Terre
, who finished fourth in the Satsuki Sho (Japanese 2000 Guineas, G1, 2,000m) and third in the Tokyo Yushun, respectively.
